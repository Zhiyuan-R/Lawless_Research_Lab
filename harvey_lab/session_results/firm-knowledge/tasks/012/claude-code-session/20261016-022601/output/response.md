# Firm Track Record: Springing-Lien Financings

## Short answer

**No. Nothing in the firm’s document set shows that we have ever closed, or even agreed to, a financing with a springing lien.** By “springing lien” I mean a provision that leaves a facility unsecured (or less secured) at closing and requires the borrower to grant liens later if a trigger occurs, such as a ratings downgrade or a leverage breach.

The one place a springing lien comes up as a deal term is Matter **1018-00008** (Penterra Pharmaceuticals / acquisition of Veridian Biosciences). There we were **borrower’s counsel**. We checked the lender’s draft credit agreement to make sure it did *not* contain a springing lien, and flagged that point as a **High** priority. That financing never funded either: the SPA was terminated and the commitment was cancelled.

Everything else the search turned up that uses “springing” language is a different mechanism: springing financial covenants, springing DACA/account control, springing cash management or lockboxes, springing recourse guaranties, springing guarantor covenants, springing maturities. Those are not precedent for a springing-lien structure, although several of them are routine in our deals (see Part 3).

**Practical point for the pitch:** we have no in-house precedent document or closed deal to point to. The nearest firm work product is the 1018-00008 memo, and it treats a springing lien as something to keep *out* of an unsecured facility. If we pitch the structure, we will be drafting it from market forms, not from our own precedent.

- **Universe:** every file in the firm document set: 9,288 files (8,055 .docx, 573 .xlsx, 615 .eml, 45 .pptx) across 266 matter folders. I converted all of them to text and searched the full text.
- **Searches run (all case-insensitive):**
  - “springing” followed by lien / first lien / security / collateral / pledge / mortgage / guarant\*. I also tallied every “springing ___” phrase in the database (about 60 distinct variants) to sort springing liens from other springing concepts.
  - Proximity searches: “spring\*” within about 80 characters of lien / security interest / collateral / pledge / mortgage, in either order.
  - Functional equivalents that don’t use the word “springing”: “lien(s) shall spring”, “springs into”, “become secured”, “secured upon”, “shall be secured … if/upon/following”, “covenant to grant/secure”, “collateral trigger”, “collateral release / reinstatement / suspension event”, “fall-away”, ratings downgrade / ratings trigger near secur\*/lien/collateral, “security trigger”, “lien trigger”.
  - Adjacent “equal and ratable” negative-pledge clauses, which people sometimes loosely call springing liens (see Part 4).
- I read each hit in context. I then read in full the documents in the one matter where a springing lien was actually addressed as a deal term (1018-00008).

## 2. The only matter where a springing lien was a deal issue: 1018-00008

**Matter:** Penterra Pharmaceuticals Corp. — Acquisition of Veridian Biosciences, Inc. (Matter No. 1018-00008). Responsible partner Oliver Mackenzie; billing partner Harrison Wexford.
**Financing:** $500,000,000 committed acquisition term loan. Summit Ridge Capital Partners, LP was Lead Arranger and Ironclad Trust Company, N.A. was Administrative Agent (Commitment Letter Ref. CL-2024-PPCA-0607, dated June 7, 2024). Pricing SOFR + 225 bps, 5-year maturity.
**Our role:** counsel to the Borrower (Penterra).

**Key document:** `matters/1018-00008/Financing/credit-agreement-markup.docx`. This is the C&H memorandum dated June 18, 2024, from Megan Chu, reviewed by Oliver Mackenzie and Harrison Wexford, giving our mark-up of the June 14, 2024 lender draft Term Loan Credit Agreement.

- The structure summary lists **“Security: Unsecured.”**
- §6.2 (Limitation on Liens), C&H Comment: *“Critically, we have verified that the draft does not contain a ‘springing lien’ provision (which would require the Borrower to grant liens upon the occurrence of specified triggers, such as a credit rating downgrade or leverage ratio breach) or an ‘equal and ratable’ provision (which would require the Borrower to grant equal liens to the Lenders if it grants liens to any other creditors). Neither of these provisions was contemplated by the Commitment Letter, and both would be inconsistent with the unsecured nature of the facility.”*
- The summary table of requested changes has this row: *“§ 6.2 — Springing lien / equal and ratable — Confirm none; facility is unsecured — High.”*
- On conditions precedent, the memo notes that the lender draft referred to “Security Documents as described in Schedule 3.2”, but that schedule was blank. It suggests lender’s counsel had used a secured template. We struck all references to security documents. The memo adds that any lender request for security would be “a material deviation from the Commitment Letter” that Penterra “should resist.”

**Outcome:** the financing never closed. `matters/1018-00008/Administrative/matter-status-memo-deal-terminated.docx` records the following:
- Penterra terminated the SPA on **September 30, 2024**, after adverse clinical data, on MAE grounds.
- A notice asking for termination of the financing commitment went to Ironclad on **October 1, 2024**.
- The loan was never drawn.

**Why this is not precedent for the pitch:**
1. We were on the *borrower* side, and our position was to *exclude* a springing lien.
2. No springing lien was ever drafted or agreed.
3. The facility never closed.

**Inconsistency in the file — check it before anyone cites this matter.** The credit-agreement mark-up memo describes the facility as **unsecured** “per the Commitment Letter.” But three other documents in the same matter describe it as a **“$500,000,000 senior secured term loan facility”** secured by a first-priority lien on substantially all assets:
- `matters/1018-00008/Financing/commitment-letter-analysis-memo.docx` (§E, “Security and Guarantees”), which also lists security and IP security agreements to be prepared;
- `matters/1018-00008/Financing/fee-letter-review-memo.docx`;
- the matter status memo.

I could not tell from the file which description is right. In both versions there is no springing lien: under one the facility is fully unsecured, under the other it is fully secured at closing. But if anyone cites this matter, the security description should be confirmed with the deal team first.

## 3. “Springing” hits that are not springing liens (not precedent)

The database uses “springing” heavily, but almost always for other mechanisms.

|---|---|---|---|

| **Springing DACA / account control** (agent gets exclusive control only after a notice following an Event of Default) | Many | 1005-00001 (closing agenda; final transaction summary memo; internal-memo-key-negotiation-points; credit agreement issues list v3, item 33); 1013-00001 (security agreement); 1008-00001 (deal summary memo); 1043-00001 (closing memorandum) | The security interest in the deposit accounts attaches at closing. | **Springing cash management / lockbox / cash sweep** | Many | 1031-00004 (cash management agreement, senior); 1036-00001 (term sheet mark-up); 1021-00007 (loan document issues memo); 1038-00002 (cash dominion) | These control how cash moves. The collateral is already in place. |

## 4. Related concept: “equal and ratable” negative pledges

Some practitioners call an equal-and-ratable negative pledge a “springing lien.” That is because unsecured debt becomes secured if the issuer grants liens to other creditors. Our own 1018-00008 memo treats these as **a different provision** from a springing lien. The firm has done this routinely in unsecured note deals. Examples:

- **1021-00002** — Verimark Hospitality Group, $500M 8.250% Senior Notes due 2031 (indenture dated June 25, 2024; senior unsecured). The Liens covenant requires the notes to be “equally and ratably secured” if VHG secures other debt beyond Permitted Liens. See the covenant analysis memo and the post-closing letter to the client.
- **1020-00003** — Greenfield Agricultural Partners, $30M Senior Unsecured Notes due 2027 (indenture draft v6, June 15, 2022). Limitation on Liens with an equal-and-ratable requirement.
- **1014-00001** — Optiwave Semiconductor, 2.75% Convertible Senior Notes due 2028 (indenture dated May 30, 2023). The successor company must secure the notes equally and ratably if a merger would subject property to a lien not otherwise permitted.

These are triggered by the issuer’s own choice to grant liens to *other* creditors. They are not triggered by credit deterioration such as a ratings downgrade or leverage breach. If the pitch is for a true springing lien, these deals are not precedent. They are useful only for drafting the related negative-pledge mechanics. If the partner’s question is meant to include equal-and-ratable clauses, the answer is yes, through these unsecured note deals.

## 5. Conclusion and recommendations

1. **Track record:** no evidence the firm has closed a financing with a springing lien triggered by ratings, leverage or another credit event. The only direct engagement with the concept (1018-00008) was a borrower-side check that the provision was *absent*, and that financing never funded.
2. **For the pitch:** do not describe the structure as something the firm has done before. We can say truthfully that we are familiar with the mechanism and its alternatives, citing:
   - the 1018-00008 analysis;
   - springing control, cash-management and recourse mechanics in secured deals (1005-00001, 1013-00001, 1031-00004, 1043-00001);
   - equal-and-ratable negative pledges in unsecured notes (1021-00002, 1020-00003, 1014-00001).
3. **Before relying on 1018-00008:** fix the unsecured-versus-secured inconsistency in that matter’s file.
4. **Gaps:** this review covers only the firm documents in the repository. Deals documented outside it, or later amendments that added a springing lien, would not appear. If the matter-management system holds more, a quick check with the finance group partners is worthwhile.
